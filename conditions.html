What this route required

  1. 1

    Could the person pass as white?

    Ellen was light enough to pass.

    If not: this route was closed.

  2. 2

    Could the person act like someone with money and rank?

    Ellen had spent years serving a doctor and his wife and knew how the powerful spoke.

  3. 3

    Would the body or voice give the disguise away?

    Ellen's smooth face and her inability to write needed cover. Woo says Ellen proposed a poultice and a sling for her right hand so that “she would have every reason to ask others to sign for her”, and another poultice for her face that gave her “added reason not to speak.”

  4. 4

    Was there a trusted companion who could play the servant?

    William was a skilled cabinetmaker with a pass to travel.

  5. 5

    Were there children?

    Woo writes that Ellen “determined that she would not marry William, not bear children—not until they escaped bondage.” The route stayed open because they had none.

  6. 6

    Were the person's family ties in one place or scattered?

    The Crafts had each been separated from family as children, so fewer ties held them back and fewer people could help them.
